# Merge Sort

**Difficulty:** intermediate · **Category:** sorting

Merge sort is a _divide-and-conquer_ algorithm. It splits the array in half, sorts each half
recursively, and then merges the two sorted halves back together. The merge step does the actual
ordering — comparing the front of each half and writing the smaller value to the output.

## What you're seeing

- The **dashed bracket** marks the sub-range currently being merged.
- **Yellow** bars are the two values being _compared_ across the two sorted halves.
- **Blue** bars are positions being _written_ as the merge proceeds left to right.
- **Green** bars are in their _final sorted position_.

Watch the bracket grow: small ranges merge first (pairs, then quads, then eights), and the
final merge spans the whole array.

## Why is it $O(n \log n)$?

Two observations:

1. **Splitting halves the problem $\log_2 n$ times.** Starting from $n$ and halving until
   you reach single elements gives you a recursion depth of $\log_2 n$.
2. **Each level of recursion does $O(n)$ work.** Across all merges at a given level, the total
   number of comparisons and writes is bounded by $n$ — every element is touched at most a
   constant number of times per level.

Multiply: $\log_2 n$ levels times $n$ work per level gives $\Theta(n \log n)$ total.

The recurrence makes this precise:

$$
T(n) = 2 T\!\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right) + \Theta(n) = \Theta(n \log n).
$$

## When is merge sort the right choice?

- **Linked lists.** Merge sort is the natural sort for linked lists — it doesn't need random
  access, and you can merge two sorted lists in $O(n)$ without any extra space.
- **External sorting.** When data doesn't fit in memory, merge sort streams over the input,
  reading and writing in sequential passes — extremely cache-friendly.
- **Stability matters.** Merge sort is _stable_ (it preserves the relative order of equal
  elements), which matters when sorting by multiple keys.

The downside: classic merge sort uses $O(n)$ auxiliary memory for the merge buffer. For sorting
arrays in place with a small constant memory footprint, quicksort or heapsort is usually
preferred.
